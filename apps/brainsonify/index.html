Just hover. Sound follows the voxel under the pointer, no clicking needed. Click still moves the crosshair if you want to change slice.

On a T1, sulci are CSF-dark and gyral crowns are gray matter, so sweeping laterally across the cortical ribbon should produce a repeating dip as you cross each sulcus.

Pitch carries intensity, the stereo image carries left–right position: the left hemisphere sounds in your left ear. The axis is anatomical, not screen-relative, so it holds while you rotate the render.

The tapping follows opacity — how much of a voxel the renderer actually shows — so dense tissue rattles and near-transparent tissue ticks. Narrow the display window and the taps saturate where the pitch is still climbing, which is the point: they are not the same signal.

The tapping follows bone, not opacity. On a T1 the skull is a signal void — the darkest, most transparent thing in the head — so opacity gets it exactly backwards. This condition finds it by shape instead: a dark sheet lying parallel to the scalp. Spike is how far the probe reaches: the tapping reports the densest bone within that distance, so resting on the scalp sounds the skull underneath it rather than the scalp itself. At 0 it reads the single voxel under the pointer, which measures honestly and is nearly impossible to hover. The rate is pushed towards its ends rather than spread evenly, because bone is a boundary rather than a quantity: sweep the vault and it should flutter almost too fast to count, sweep the brain and it should barely tick. An orange line on the 2D tiles marks where the probe actually found that bone, since “densest within reach” is not always the voxel under the pointer.

On a whole-head scan the scalp and skull wrap everything, so hovering the 3D render can only ever sound the outside. Clip cuts the near side away and puts the interior under the pointer. The cut follows the camera, so it keeps facing you as you rotate.

Rate speeds the whole rhythm up without changing the ratios in it. Two slow rates take several taps each to tell apart, which is longer than the pointer usually stays still; the same ratio played faster is legible at once. Taps only drops the tone entirely, so nothing competes with the rhythm while you learn what it is saying.

White noise drops the pitch channel entirely: brightness (a lowpass cutoff) carries intensity instead, so there is no tone for the taps to compete with. Meant to make the rhythm easier to hear on its own, in the background, rather than as a texture riding on top of a pitch that is also moving.

The region under the pointer is named out loud when you enter it, from the AAL atlas, over whatever else is sounding. Rest in a region to hear it; a sweep straight across says nothing, so the names do not pile up. The atlas is in MNI space: it is right for the MNI152 demo and for any scan already in that space, and it is switched off for the whole-head T1, which is not.

Wear the headset. Try Filtered noise or White noise if the pure tone feels too abstract.

Starting a session

Five steps before the listener starts exploring. The listener cannot see this screen, so read the words in quotes out loud.

  1. Load the image

    The MNI152 demo loads by itself, and region names only work on it or another MNI-space scan. Swap it here, or drop a .nii / .nii.gz file on the page later.

    Loading the MNI152 demo…

  2. Fit the headphones

    Put the headset on the listener now, both sides seated, before any sound starts. Left and right is one of the channels and only reads with two ears. Start quiet: Volume in the panel raises it once they are listening.

  3. Hand over the Helm

    The Sonaview Helm is the listener's; its buttons are yours. Until the rotary panel arrives the keyboard stands in for it: ↑ ↓ turn, Enter presses. Keep the mode keys (1–5, s, n, Home) on your side.

  4. Explain the Helm

    “Turning the Helm moves you through the scan, and the sound changes with what is under you. I will choose what turning moves, and you will hear it announced each time I change it. Pressing the Helm tells you where you are and never changes anything, so press it whenever you lose track. First you will hear a short key that explains each sound.”

  5. Start the session

    Start session cuts the scan down the middle, front from back, shown at an angle in the 3D render, then turns the sound on and plays the sound key for this condition. Let it finish before the listener turns or hovers; it stops at the first move. The condition can be changed in the panel afterwards. When the patient is done, End session in the panel clears their scan and brings these steps back for the next one.
